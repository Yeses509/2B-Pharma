Our Code of Conduct turns our five values into working rules for everyone who acts for Two Brothers Health. In summary:

Guides & Explainers

Working guides for importers, distributors and pharmacy buyers — how the processes actually run, in plain language. General educational content: your market’s own rules always apply.

GuideImporting healthcare products from the United States: documents, licences and timelines+

Importing medicines from the US is a documentation exercise before it is a logistics one. The shipment itself is the easy part; what decides whether it clears smoothly is the paper that travels with it and the licences held on both ends.

Documents that travel with a typical export shipment — every 2B shipment carries this pack; the canonical list is in Logistics & cold chain:

  • Commercial invoice and packing list — what is in the shipment, in what quantities, at what declared value.
  • Certificate of origin — where the goods were made.
  • Free-sale certificate / certificate of healthcare product — evidence the product is authorised for sale in its home market, where the destination requires it.
  • Certificate of analysis — the batch’s test results against its specification.
  • Temperature data-logger report — for refrigerated products, the recorded proof the cold chain held.

Licences: the exporter must be licensed to sell and ship medicines; the destination import licence and, where required, product registration sit with the importer unless agreed otherwise in writing.

Timelines: there is no honest single number. If the product is already registered in your market, an order moves at the speed of production and freight; if registration is still needed, that process — run by your market’s authority — sets the calendar. A good supplier confirms your timeline in writing at quotation, before you commit.

GuideCold chain for exporters+

Many medicines must be kept refrigerated between 2°C and 8°C from the factory to the pharmacy shelf — the instruction is printed on the carton itself (“Store at 2–8°C”). The cold chain is the set of controls that keeps that promise across warehouses, trucks, ports and aircraft holds.

  • Storage & packaging: stock is held in temperature-mapped storage and shipped in insulated packaging validated for the lane and season — proven, not hoped, to hold temperature for the journey. (Our own process: Logistics & cold chain.)
  • Data loggers: a recorder travels with the shipment and logs temperature throughout; its report is the evidence, and it should arrive with the shipment’s documents.
  • Excursions: if a temperature limit is breached, the stock is quarantined — never quietly sold on — and the logger record is assessed before any decision about the goods.

When you evaluate any supplier, ask one question: “Show me the logger report from my last shipment.” A real cold chain produces that document as a matter of routine.

GuideHow to verify a US healthcare wholesaler+

Before you place a first order with any US healthcare wholesaler or exporter, verify them the way regulators expect you to. It takes a few checks, and a legitimate supplier will welcome every one.

  • Ask for the licences: a genuine wholesaler holds state licensing for healthcare wholesale distribution and can share licence documentation during onboarding. Vagueness here is a stop sign.
  • Check the company is real: a registered company, a physical address you can find, and people who answer questions directly — including the questions they cannot say yes to.
  • Ask about traceability: can they provide batch numbers, transaction documentation and certificates of analysis with shipments? Do their packs carry scannable traceability codes?
  • Test the paperwork on a small order: invoice, packing list, certificate of origin and analysis should arrive complete and consistent with the goods.

Red flags: prices far below the market with no explanation, refusal to share licence documentation, pressure to pay quickly or in unusual ways, no physical address, and product offered to unlicensed buyers or the general public. Walk away from all five.

ExplainerDSCSA in plain English+

The DSCSA — the US Drug Supply Chain Security Act — is the federal law that makes prescription medicines traceable, package by package, through the US supply chain. Its goal is simple: any unit of medicine should be traceable back through every hand it passed through, so falsified or diverted product can be found and removed.

  • Serialisation: each saleable package carries a unique serial number, encoded in a scannable 2D (DataMatrix) code alongside the product identifier, lot number and expiry date.
  • Transaction data: as product changes hands, trading partners exchange electronic transaction information — what the product is, and its history of ownership (often exchanged in the standard EPCIS format).
  • Verification: if a package is suspect, its serial number can be checked back against the manufacturer’s data to confirm it is genuine.
  • Authorised trading partners: the law works because every party in the chain — manufacturer, wholesaler, pharmacy — must be licensed and may only trade with other authorised partners.

For export buyers, the practical meaning is this: product that has moved through the US authorised supply chain carries a documented pedigree — batch, lot and transaction history — which is exactly the evidence your own regulators want to see.

We notify affected customers directly, with the product and lot details and clear instructions. We ask for written acknowledgement that you received the notice; affected stock is quarantined and returned as instructed, for credit or replacement under the recall terms.

What is your returns policy?+

Returns need prior authorisation — contact us first, with the product, lot and reason. Unopened, in-date product is assessed case by case; expired or temperature-compromised product never returns to saleable stock — it is quarantined and destroyed safely. The full picture is in our Returns & reverse logistics service.

What happens if a shipment has a temperature excursion on arrival?+

Do not use or distribute the stock. Quarantine it, keep the packaging and the data logger, and contact us — our quality team assesses the logger record and advises on the goods. Excursions are exactly what the logger travels for.

How do you keep falsified or diverted product out of your supply?+

Licensed sourcing only, bought direct from manufacturers wherever possible, with batch verification before release and traceability on every carton — see Quality & compliance. If a pack’s DataMatrix code or hologram seal looks wrong, treat it as suspect and tell us.

Suppliers
How do we become a supplier — or appoint 2B as distributor for our products?+

Start with Become a supplier: tell us your range, your markets and your authorisations through Contact, and we will open the conversation. We appoint and are appointed on the strength of long-term partnership, not one-off deals.

What due-diligence documents do you require from suppliers? Do you audit?+

In general terms: company registration, manufacturing or wholesale licences, product authorisations and quality documentation. Verification is part of onboarding, and audits or evaluations may form part of it where appropriate — ongoing expectations are set out in our Supplier Code.

What are your receiving, labelling and packaging requirements for suppliers?+

Every carton must carry clear batch, manufacturing and expiry labelling and traceability coding, in packaging that protects the product and its cold chain. The detailed specification for your products is confirmed in the supplier agreement and onboarding pack.

Compliance, careers & general
Where can I find your licences, Code of Conduct and quality commitments?+

Our commitments and policy summaries are published in Trust & policies on this site. Specific licence documentation is shared with verified trade partners during onboarding, as part of mutual due diligence.

How can I raise an ethics or compliance concern — and can I stay anonymous?+

Through the Contact form, marked as a compliance concern — see Speak up in Trust & policies. Anonymity is respected where lawful, every concern is reviewed, and raising one in good faith will never be held against you.

Do you offer grants, donations or sponsorships?+

We do not currently run a grants, donations or sponsorship programme, and we would rather say so plainly here than leave it to enquiry. Our wider commitments are described under Responsibility.

How do I apply for a job? Who do media contact?+

Current openings are listed under Careers — when there are none, you are welcome to send your CV through Contact and we will keep it on file. Media enquiries also go through Contact, marked as a media enquiry.
